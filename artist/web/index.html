The drawing

The drawing. Your strokes are clipped to the canvas and rasterised one pixel wide, which is the target the artist is given. The artist starts with an empty canvas and a pose somewhere over it. Every tenth of a second it commits one of eighteen choices, nine torque pairs by the pen up or down, and the pen leaves a one-pixel line along the path it swept with the pen down. The discrepancy is the symmetric Chamfer distance between the two foregrounds in canvas diagonals, and the F1 counts a drawn pixel as matched when a target pixel lies within one pixel of it. A drawing ends under a discrepancy of 0.010, at 200 decisions, or after 60 decisions without a better discrepancy.

The decisions. Two levels of supplied search read one learned model. The slow level asks the records what one decision of motion leaves for each of eight directions and both pen states, repeats that stamp along a candidate stroke, scores the canvas it imagines by the capped discrepancy, and keeps the best stroke over a beam of four and a horizon of two. That stroke travels in the goal port. The fast level imagines all eighteen choices two decisions deep, scores the ink each is expected to leave and how well the imagined hand velocity follows the stroke, and the agent executes the best. The intention is renewed every second decision from the canvas the artist sees, so the plan follows what the pen actually left.

The brain. Sensory and goal neurons drive the workspace; the workspace couples to the dynamics cortex, which reads the efference copy of the action; the motor neurons hang off the workspace. The world model is the records cortex beside the scan: the body senses, the pen state, the ink of the last decision, the canvas and target windows around the pen, the intention and the candidate action go through a fixed random expansion, the most active granules are kept, and each active cell's records of the next displacement change, velocity change, angle change and ink window are read out. When the outcome arrives it is written into exactly those records, so the brain keeps learning while it draws. The grey flickers on the raster are the search reading records it never writes.
