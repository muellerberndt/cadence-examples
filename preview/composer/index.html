What this brain does

  • Learned the chip by playing it. What a gesture of 28 register controls does to the sound is in its records, written block by block from what it heard.
  • Answers a phrase it has just heard, note by note, by searching over what it imagines each candidate would sound like.
  • Hears voice by voice. The phrase is played to it once per voice and then whole.
  • One pass over one stream. No replay ring, no backward pass, no training step.
  • Runs in this page. The chip is WebAssembly, the ear, the records and the search are JavaScript, decision for decision equal to the Python stage.
  • Does not pass its gates. The numbers are below, the open ones marked.

What is supplied and what is learned

Supplied: the emulated SID and its clock; the control-to-register codec and its bin tables; the 68 hearing features a channel (64 log bands from 100 Hz to 10 kHz, the level, the onset, a silence flag) on the mix and on each voice's own monitor; the pitch reader's comb; the tasks, their phases and their rewards; the search that proposes a one-semitone grid around the note the reader names. Learned: what a gesture does to the sound, which is the records cortex of 4,000 cells with 60 active over a reading of 957, and every representation between the hearing and the gesture.

How this page runs the brain

A decision takes no settling: the search reads the records cortex and nothing else, so what the scan draws is the drive that decision reads, the hearing on the sensory ports and the efference copy of the gesture on the motor ports. The records view under it is the cells that reading lights, with the reading itself under them. The chip is the pinned reSIDfp built to WebAssembly, and it reproduces the emulator the stage renders with, sample for sample: 529,233 samples over three fixtures, none of them different. A decision in this page matches the stage's on 6,744 recorded decisions, with five differences, every one of them a block where the candidates cost exactly the same. Learning is off here and nothing writes to the records, so what the brain knows does not move while you listen to it.

The material and the licence

The seven phrases are the stage's own sample pieces, written for it and placed in the public domain. The corpus this brain learned from stays private: HVSC register performances and renderings of Lakh MIDI arrangements, which carry their own rights. The chip is reSIDfp from libsidplayfp through pyresidfp 0.17.0, which is GPL-2.0-or-later, so this page is a derivative work of GPL sources and is served under the same licence: LICENSE is the GNU General Public License version 2, and source/ carries the corresponding source of what is served, which is the pinned 0.17.0 archive, the reproducibility patch, the thread rewrite, the shim and the build script.
