What this brain can do

    What it sees, and what it is not given

    Four fields and nothing else: the displayed frame as a 64 by 64 grayscale view, area-averaged from the full screen; that view less the view of the frame before it; the fraction of the last action's four frames each of the nine NES buttons was down; and two numbers, the gameplay frames so far and the frames one action lasts. No RAM, no score, no lives, no stage, no plane coordinates and no audio. The two small canvases on the screen are the first two fields at their own resolution, and the screen behind them is what a person watching the cabinet sees.

    What it does with the pad

    Nine directions, which are neutral, the four cardinals and the four diagonals, by fire on or off, which is 18 categorical actions. Each action lasts four emulated frames. Directions are held for all four. Fire holds B on the first two frames and releases it on the last two, because tapping fires more shots than holding in 1942: 20 shots in 600 frames against 13 for a held button. The masks come from the run manifest and are never re-derived here.

    Supplied and learned

      The instrumentation

      The score, the deaths, the stage, the plane's position and the frames in play are read from emulator RAM through one declared door, Adapter.privileged_ram, by the evaluator alone. A run whose training reward is built from that door is "pixels plus supplied reward" and says so. Two further bytes, 0x48E and 0x432, carry the episode boundary and the end of the startup routine, and they are the only RAM the adapter itself reads.
