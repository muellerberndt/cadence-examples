Grey Parrots

Two African greys in one room. Each has a cochlea, a syrinx with three muscles, and one equilibrium net for a brain that you can watch settle and learn: when a sound begins, a chain of owners lights up moment by moment (the parrot's clock, keyed to the sound's first 160 ms), the memory draws what it expects to hear at each moment, the mirror answers with the muscle command that would make it, and the seams that move on each update glow. Play the household to them, make a noise of your own and repeat it, let one speak and the other will learn from it.

How to read the brain

Top block: the sequence owners, the parrot's chain. Each is a fixed random conjunction of the cue (the sound's first 160 ms after lateral inhibition) and the clock; the few that fire at a given moment of a given sound are its address in the memory, and the seams from them to the expected frame are what listening writes. Below: what the mirror reads of the last 40 ms (the peaks after lateral inhibition, the loudness, and the dominant channel as a bump, a pitch code), the vocal population under the mirror, the cochlear frame the memory expects (24 channels, low to high), and the three muscles as bumps over their owners (tension, pressure, tract). Amber is activation; a red halo is a seam that just moved. The body: the beak opens with pressure, the throat swells with the tract setting, the head tilts to a sound.
