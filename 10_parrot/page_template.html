Grey Parrot

One equilibrium net is this parrot's brain: it hears a household through a cochlea, keeps what it hears often as a memory of what comes next, and moves a physical syrinx through a mirror it learned by babbling. Play the sounds it grew up with, let it speak, or draw a whistle and repeat it until the parrot picks it up. Everything here settles and learns in the page.

The household

Each play reaches the cochlea and the memory learns from it, as it did all day. Recall is how well the memory, replayed from the sound's first 80 ms, reproduces the rest (correlation; 1 is perfect).

Facts

How it works

Cochlea: a bank of second-order band-pass filters, an RMS envelope per 10 ms, log-compressed. Brain: 480 auditory owners (the last eight frames, six bins of 50 ms and six of 200 ms behind them, whose fill is the parrot's clock) to 160 hidden owners to 40 output owners by tied seams; 24 outputs are the expected next frame, 16 are population codes for three muscles. Learning: settle free, settle with the output group pulled toward its target (and once pushed away), move each seam by the difference of the two Hebbian products; seams decay a little every update. Syrinx: the Amador–Mindlin labial oscillator into a one-resonance tract, integrated at 128 kHz. The parrot never sees a label; the names on this page are for you.
