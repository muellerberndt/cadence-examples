Grey Parrots

Two African greys in one room. Each has a cochlea, a syrinx with three muscles, and one equilibrium net for a brain that you can watch settle and learn: the owners light up as it listens, the memory group draws what it expects next, the mirror answers with muscle commands, and the seams that move on each update glow. Play the household to them, make a noise of your own and repeat it, let one speak and the other will learn from it.

The household what they grew up hearing

Each play reaches both cochleas and both memories learn from it. Recall is how well a memory, replayed from the sound's first 80 ms, reproduces the rest (1 is perfect).

How to read the brain

Top block: the context, 24 cochlear channels across (low to high) by 20 rows of time: six bins of 200 ms, six of 50 ms, then the last eight frames, newest at the bottom. Middle: the auditory population (128 owners) under the memory and the vocal population (96) under the mirror. Bottom rows: the expected next frame, and the three muscles as bumps over their owners (tension, pressure, tract). Amber is activation; a red halo is a seam that just moved. The body: the beak opens with pressure, the throat swells with the tract setting, the head tilts to a sound.
