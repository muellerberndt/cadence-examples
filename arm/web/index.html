What this brain can do

  • Learned its own body from random motor babbling. No kinematics were written into it.
  • Copies a figure it has never seen by imagining what each torque would do and taking the one whose motion follows your line.
  • Keeps learning while it copies. The second copy of a figure is usually closer than the first.
  • Adapts to a changed body with no reset. Lengthen the upper link by 15% and it recovers; restore the body and it returns.
  • One pass over one stream of experience. No replay ring, no backward pass, no training switch.
  • Runs entirely in your browser, decision for decision equal to the Python agent.

What plays here

A two-link arm and one Cadence brain. Sensory and goal neurons drive a workspace; the workspace couples to a dynamics cortex that reads the efference copy of the action; motor neurons hang off the workspace; context neurons carry a trace of the last workspace state. The world model is a records cortex (cerebellum-like): the reading passes through a fixed random expansion, the most active cells keep it, and each active cell holds a record of what followed. Every settling step of the brain is drawn as it happens.

Measured

Acceptance receipt, five seeds. Held-out reaching 99% · copier tracking error 0.052 arm lengths · after a longer link 99%, back on the original body 99%. Same life, world model swapped: online MLP 2% without replay and 98% with a ring · online transformer 40% without replay and 100% with a ring · Jacobian controller 94% · random torques 0%. Learning curve of a read-only copy: 22% after 3,000 decisions, 60% after 6,000, 87% after 10,000.

How it works

Your strokes are resampled, clipped to the reachable ring and played as a moving target. Every torque comes from the brain's step: the planner imagines each of nine torque pairs through the records cortex (the blue fan at the hand), scores the imagined motion against the target, and the brain executes the best. The outcome of every decision is written into exactly the records that read it, so the brain keeps learning while it copies. The exploration slider sets the chance of a random torque instead of the planner's choice.
