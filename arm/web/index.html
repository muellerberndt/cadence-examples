Body

The copy. Your strokes are resampled to even spacing, smoothed a little, clipped to the reachable ring and played as a moving target. The target waits at the first point until the hand arrives (at most six seconds), then advances one point per decision at the chosen speed; between strokes it travels in a straight line with the pen lifted. The ink is the hand's own path at the body's 50 Hz clock. The tracking error is the mean distance between the hand and the target over the moving part of the copy, in arm lengths, and the arm's full reach is one.

The decisions. Every torque comes from the agent's step. The planner imagines each of the nine torque pairs held for three decisions through the records cortex (the blue fan at the hand), scores the imagined hand motion against a velocity toward the target, and the agent executes the best torque. The outcome of every decision is written into the records of the cells that read it, so the brain keeps learning while it copies, and a second copy of the same figure is usually closer than the first. The exploration slider sets the probability of a random torque instead of the planner's choice; it starts at zero.

The brain. Sensory and goal neurons drive the workspace; the workspace couples to the dynamics cortex, which reads the efference copy of the action; the motor neurons hang off the workspace; the context neurons carry a trace of the last workspace state. The world model is the records cortex beside the scan (cerebellum-like, dentate-like): the reading of the sensory, goal and action neurons goes through a fixed random expansion, the most active granules are kept (the lit cells), and each active cell's records of the next displacement change, velocity change and angle change are read out; when the outcome arrives it is written into exactly those records (the flash). Every settling step of every phase of the settled regions is drawn as it happens. The brain selector loads other points of this arm's life when the page is served with its checkpoint manifest.
