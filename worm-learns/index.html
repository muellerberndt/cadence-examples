C. elegans · learning on the published wiring

Is dinner under the diacetyl or the butanone?

A worm starts with the 300-neuron Cook 2019 connectome and no idea which smell leads to food. Every step, Cadence's local rule adjusts the published synapses into its chemotaxis circuit, and the reward is food. Watch the turn bars pull apart, then swap the smells and watch it relearn.

Measured, not staged

Python runs with the same rule, 16 worms per update, three seeds each, scored on 1,000 dishes they never trained on. The shuffled control keeps every neuron's senses, motor role and synapse counts but rewires who talks to whom: none of its seeds reached 90% within 8,000 decisions. A small MLP learns faster than either.

Remove AWA or RIA from a trained worm and it falls to chance; remove AWC and nothing changes. Swap the food smell and all three seeds relearn within 3,500–7,000 decisions. Afterwards AWC matters and AWA no longer does, and RIA is still needed.

On this page one worm learns alone, so learning from naive takes about 40,000 decisions, around three minutes at Fastest. Load the trained brain to skip ahead.

Supplied, not learned: the dish, the two odour clouds, AWA and AWC reading a left/right comparison of their odour at the head, and a step body in which SMDD turns left, SMDV turns right and AVB moves forward. Learning changes the strength of 941 published synapses into the chemotaxis circuit, each bounded at three times its published strength, rewarded by food and by moving closer to it. Without that bound, one turning neuron latches on and the worm circles. This is not a validated model of worm olfactory learning.
