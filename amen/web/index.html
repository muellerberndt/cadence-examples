The brain

One Cadence record patch. 80 input ports (a wake, an eight-position clock, and the 71 ports of the event it played last), 128 context channels whose time constants run from 2 to 128 half-beats, one linear readout to 71 output ports, 29,895 slow parameters, and a record store of 8,192 cells of which 48 fire per reading. The records hold what the slow readout got wrong at each reading, keyed by the event heard and the context; the first four bars of a new track are written into them while the slow parameters stay fixed. Trained in under seven CPU minutes on a laptop, no GPU.

What plays here

Every clip is the literal render of what the brain played: one slice of a drum break per half-beat at the predicted gain, sub-bass notes from C1 to B2, and a texture of band-passed noise following eight predicted bands. In the clips marked "the beat held" it plays its highest-scoring event; in the clips "with departures" it leaves the loop only where it predicts a change point, by a roll (the slice just heard repeats) or a retrigger (the break restarts at a bar start). In the shaded first four bars you hear the transcription of the unheard track through the same instrument, not the brain. Every generated bar was checked against the training corpus: none occurs there verbatim.

Measured on three tracks it never trained on

  • Next drum slice: 0.84 to 0.86 correct; the most frequent slice scores 0.03 to 0.05.
  • Next bass note: 0.54 to 0.64; repeating the previous note scores 0.14 to 0.48.
  • Texture bands: mean error 0.085 to 0.090; repeating the previous half-beat 0.099 to 0.117.
  • Change points are hard: recall 0.07 to 0.15, with a predicted probability about twice as high at true change points as elsewhere.

Supplied, learned, limits

Supplied: the transcription of the recordings into events (which slice, which note, where a bar departs from the bar before, the sustain above 250 Hz), the instrument that renders events, the clock, and the two moves a departure may make. Learned: everything the brain plays, from random parameters and empty records. Limits: one mix of sixteen training tracks, one seed, no transformer or recurrent baseline on the same stream yet, and a texture smoother than the real one because a squared-error readout predicts the mean.
