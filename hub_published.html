Six separate experiments. Each opens with its live circuit beside the task, so you can see the state that drives behavior.

01 / VISION & MOTOR CONTROL

Your drawing. Its pencil.

Draw on a pad. An eye reads the pixels. Motor neurons move two joints and raise or lower the pencil to copy what it sees.

Open eye & arm → 02 / MEMORY & NAVIGATION

Teach a mouse a new destination.

Task memory recalls the goal, a spatial field finds a route, and directional motor neurons move the body. Change a corridor or teach another task.

Open teachable mouse → 03 / CHEMICAL SENSING

Feed a worm. Build its world.

Place food and draw barriers. Follow a public C. elegans chemical circuit and its engineered directional motor system.

Open worm habitat → 04 / LEARNING THROUGH CONTACT

Find nectar. Remember the encounter.

A forager learns nectar values after contact, recalls useful flowers and steers with motor activity. Change the rewards while it explores.

Open forager → 05 / ASSOCIATIVE MEMORY

A new observation changes a record.

Inspect the memory mechanism used by the embodied examples. Compare one residual write with online MLP updates on the same observations.

Open changing memory → 06 / IMAGINATION & SELF-MONITORING

Play against a reasoner.

Connect four while the agent explores replies, compares futures and reads its own uncertainty to decide whether to think deeper.
